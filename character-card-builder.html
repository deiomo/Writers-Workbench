Character Card Builder / SillyTavern
Identity

The basics. Goal here is the public one — what they're openly working toward. The private version goes in Personal goal further down; when the two differ, that gap is a free source of tension.

Appearance & vibe

Concrete and specific over flattering. The most useful line is usually what people get wrong about them at a glance — it gives the model a way to introduce them that isn't a checklist.

Backstory

History only earns its place if it changes how they act now. If a fact from their past never alters a decision they make in a scene, cut it — it's tokens the model can't use.

Abilities & skills

What they can do, and where it costs them or fails. A limit is worth more to a model than a strength — strengths get invoked to solve everything, limits create scenes.

Personality tags

Keep them distinct — if two tags could describe the same behaviour, drop one. Tags are the summary; the triggers below are what actually drive behaviour, so don't stack a trait here that isn't backed by a trigger.

Psychology & behavioral triggers

One trigger per row. Written as When X happens, they do Y — a condition and an observable behaviour, not a feeling.

Core contradiction

Two things that are both true and pull against each other. If it doesn't create a decision they'd struggle with, it isn't a contradiction yet.

Intimacy & vulnerability

Which way they lean, what pulls them, and the one line they hold. Friction points do more work than preferences — a preference tells the model what to write, a friction point gives it somewhere to go.

Hobbies & interests

What they do when no one needs anything from them. This keeps scenes from collapsing into the plot discussing itself — it gives them something to talk about that isn't the relationship or the mission.

Quirks

The irrational one works best — something they do consistently and can't justify.

Personal goal

The private want — the thing they'd only admit to one person, or to no one. If it matches the public Goal at the top exactly, one of them is probably unnecessary.

Relationships

Format: Who they are — what the dynamic actually is.

Voice & example dialogue

Tone in a few words, then three to five lines that show the voice across different moods — flustered, angry, relaxed, lying. Example dialogue enforces voice far better than a paragraph describing it ever will.

Exports as a SillyTavern World Info .json. Each character becomes one entry, keyed on their name.

Side characters don't need a full psychology. They need one clear reason to be in the scene, one thing they want, and a voice that isn't interchangeable with the next NPC. Everything here is optional — a good side card can be six lines.

About the token counts

Token numbers shown next to each section are estimates, not real tokenizer output. This file runs offline with no tokenizer library, so it approximates from character and word counts. Expect it to land within roughly 10–15% of a real BPE count for ordinary English prose, and to drift further on heavy punctuation, unusual names, or non-English text. Use it to compare sections against each other, not as an exact budget.

Using it

  • Type the name once — it fills every heading and possessive in the output.
  • Every field is optional. Empty sections are dropped from the output unless you turn off Hide empty sections.
  • Both card tabs hold a roster — switch between characters with the chips at the top. The side tab Add as many characters as a scenario needs, then export the whole roster as a SillyTavern World Info .json — one entry per character, keyed on their name. SillyTavern takes the lorebook name from the filename on import.
  • Save .json stores the whole roster of the tab you're on. Loading a roster file replaces that tab's roster; loading an older single-card file adds it as a new entry. There is no autosave.
  • Ctrl/Cmd + S saves the current tab as .json.
  • Runs offline. The font is the only thing loaded from the internet, and it falls back cleanly without one.
